## 1. The one-paragraph summary

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What do you put on **your servers**? | **Very little**: an identity public key, a pseudonymous nickname / mask, and the **hashes** in the public append-only log (no plaintext). At runtime we also see your **source IP** - used only for rate limiting and abuse protection, **held in memory only and never written to the database** (see §2). |
| Do you hold my private key? | **No.** The private key **never leaves the device** — there is no "type your signature here" field in the client UI, and no endpoint on the server that accepts a private key. |
| Can you read what I talked about from the log? | **No.** The transparency log stores **leaf hashes** only (irreversible digests), never the conversation text. |
| Can I export / delete it? | **Yes.** Local data can be **encrypted and exported** as a single archive, and deleted; server-side identity data can be deleted on request (see §5). |

## 2. What we collect, why, and where it lives

| Data | Purpose | Where it is stored | Deletable |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Identity public key** (Ed25519, no private key) | Lets others verify that "this record really was signed by you" | Server (Hub) | Yes (deleted / revoked on account closure) |
| **Nickname / mask profile** | Display name; **a real identity is not required** | Server | Yes |
| **Transparency-log leaf hashes** | §16 verifiability: let anyone check the log was not rewritten | Server (**publicly downloadable**) | No (append-only is the whole point; but **no plaintext is in them**) |
| **Published skill listings** (volunteered by the author) | Attribution and pricing in the skill market | Server | Yes, delistable (history retained so signatures can still be checked) |
| **End-to-end encrypted messages** | Communication between users | The server stores **ciphertext** only; the key stays on the device | Yes |
| **Model-usage metering** (tokens, cost) | Billing (trial credit / author revenue share) | Server | Retained with the ledger |
| **Source IP** | Rate limiting and abuse protection: per-IP requests per minute, per-IP trial-credit allocation | **Memory only**, in the rate-limit counters; the runtime log records a **truncated network prefix** only (e.g. `1.2.3.0/24`), never the full IP | Cleared on process restart; counters are deleted by the periodic sweep once their window expires (see below) |

**How long the source IP is kept (numbers, not "a short period")**:

| Form | Kept for | What removes it |
|---|---|---|
| Per-minute rate-limit counters (requests per minute, per-minute trial-credit allocation) | **At most 2 hours**: 60-second windows, swept every 10 minutes; a key is deleted once it has had no access for more than 2 windows | The in-process periodic sweep; a process restart clears them immediately |
| Per-day trial-credit counters | **At most 25 hours**: deleted by the first sweep after the day rolls over | Same as above |
| The network prefix in the runtime log | This software **writes to stdout only and keeps no log file**; if the deployer redirects it to a file, that **deployer** decides the retention period | The deployer |

★ The full IP **is never written to any persistent file**: it only serves as a counter key in memory.
★ The source IP **is not used for profiling, is not stored in the same record as your identity, and is not used for advertising**.

**We do not collect**: your address book, precise location, a device advertising identifier, your real name or identity documents, or payment card numbers (**we never handle your card number**: even when a paid plan exists, payment is processed by a licensed payment provider; money flows are described in §4 of the Terms of Service).

**Retention of the transparency log (stated as numbers, not as "long term")**:

| Form | How long it is kept | Who deletes it |
|---|---|---|
| **Leaf hashes** (§16 verifiability) | **Permanently**: append-only, not deletable — this is the price of verifiability | No deletion path exists (nor in the code) |
| **The publicly downloadable log file itself** | The official Hub currently does **not rotate or delete** it; for a self-hosted Hub this is decided by that operator | The operator |

★ Keeping leaf hashes **permanently** is the price of verifiability, not an oversight: once deletion is allowed, no one can independently recompute that "the log was not tampered with".
★ They contain **no plaintext** (only one-way digests), so what is kept permanently is not the content of your messages.

### 2.1 Self-hosted Hub: who is the controller of your data

The Fen client can connect to a **Hub set up by you or by a third party**. To be clear:

- **The operator of that Hub (not us) is the controller of its data**: what it stores, how long it keeps it and who it is shared with is decided and owned by that operator.
- **We never touch** any data on a self-hosted Hub and **cannot** delete or export it for you - contact the operator of that server directly to exercise your rights.
- ⇒ So the collection scope in the §2 table applies **only to the Hub we operate**; the rights listed in §5 must likewise be claimed against that operator on a self-hosted Hub.

## 3. AI use and disclosure

- The **agents / representatives on the platform may be driven by AI**. Per PRD §9.2 we use **product-level disclosure plus first-turn self-identification**: the very first interaction with an AI states that it is an AI, and that disclosure is **on by default and cannot be turned off**.
- If you use the "trial credit" (the platform proxies the model call for you), the call is made on your behalf and the result is returned to you; we keep an interaction log so we can evidence our disclosure duty, but we **do not profile you and do not use it for advertising**.
- **Trial credit is limited (20 turns by default) and is for evaluation only**: while it lasts, your conversation is relayed through our server to a model provider, and the route is chosen by **the region you connect from** (as recorded by the server). Once the credit runs out, entering your own model key lets you continue — from then on the call **does not pass through us**, nor through the trial relay.

| Region you connect from | Relayed to | Where your conversation goes |
|---|---|---|
| Within the Chinese mainland | A model provider located there (for example Alibaba Cloud Bailian / Volcano Engine) | **Stays within the Chinese mainland** |
| Outside the Chinese mainland | OpenRouter, Inc. (United States) | **Does not enter the Chinese mainland** |

The route is chosen by region so that each leg sits inside the legal regime that actually applies to it — which is not the same thing as blocking access by region. **We do not currently refuse access because of where you are, but we reserve the right to restrict access in specific regions where the law requires it.**
- If you supply your own model key in the client, the call **does not pass through us** and we have no way to know its contents.

## 4. What data minimisation means in practice (engineering evidence)

These are not slogans; they are facts you can check in the code:

| Promise | Where to verify |
|---|---|
| The private key does not leave the device | `lib/core/agent_protocol.dart` (signing happens locally; there is no upload path) |
| The log holds no plaintext | `lib/core/transparency_log.dart` / `lib/hub/hub.js` §16: only `leaf_hash` is stored |
| The log is publicly downloadable | `GET /api/log/leaves` (anonymous read) |
| Local data is exportable | `lib/core/local_archive.dart` + `lib/core/archive_format.dart` (passphrase-encrypted `.dahuang`, **without the private key**; import checks §16 log continuity first, see §5) |
| Trial credit is tiny and purpose-limited | `lib/core/trial_gateway.dart` (no proxying by default + allow-list + credit recorded in the database) |
| The source IP is never persisted; the log keeps a prefix only | `lib/hub/hub.js`: `maskIp()` truncates the log, `sweepRateTables()` sweeps the counters |

## 5. Your rights (access / export / correction / deletion)

| Right | How to exercise it |
|---|---|
| **Access / export** | In the client, "Export local data" produces a **passphrase-encrypted** archive (`.dahuang`). |
| **Correction** | The nickname / mask can be changed at any time in settings. |
| **Deletion** | ① Local data: clear it in one tap; ② server-side identity data: see below. |
| **Withdraw consent / close the account** | Send a closure request to privacy@wold.world. |

**On export (what it actually is)**:

- The archive is encrypted with **a passphrase you set** (the same scheme as the recovery code), and **we cannot open it and never handle it**.
  Keep it somewhere **you** consider safe.
- The archive contains **your local data** (identity records, social graph, introduction records, transparency-log anchors and so on),
  but ★ **not your private key** — the private key backup is a different thing (see §2.1 "recovery code"), and the two are **kept separately**.
- ⚠️ **What you get is a file, not a sync**: we do not offer cloud sync. Where that file goes afterwards, and whether you can find it again, is your decision.
- The transparency log comes with a **continuity anchor**: when you import the archive on another device, if the two logs do not agree, the client **refuses the import** and tells you why (this is the other face of "the log cannot be quietly rewritten").

**An honest note on deletion**: the **hashes** in the transparency log cannot be deleted — that is the necessary price of verifiability, i.e. of anyone being able to check that history was not rewritten. But note:

> **The hashes in the log are one-way digests and contain no plaintext at all.** Once your identity mapping is deleted, that hash can no longer be linked to "you".

We commit to: closure requests are processed within **30 days** (unless the applicable law requires otherwise).

## 8. Security

- TLS throughout in transit; identity signatures use Ed25519.
- The server **does not hold** users' private keys, so even a compromised server cannot sign as any user.
- Data breach: once confirmed, we will notify affected users and the supervisory authority (as required by applicable law) within **72 hours**, through privacy@wold.world / an in-product notice.

### 8.1 Notifications and push: how data flows

**Current version: notifications are generated on-device and do not pass through any server.**

- The notifications the client shows (a delegation arriving, someone @-ing you in the parlour, a draft suggestion card waiting for your approval, and so on) are **all generated and displayed locally on your device**, triggered by the in-app event bus (`AppEvents`), and **do not pass through our servers or any third-party push channel**.
- Where to verify: `lib/core/notification_service.dart` (no http / network call anywhere in the file); the background duty heartbeat runs in a separate isolate and re-installs the same set of listeners inside that isolate, purely so background events can also surface - **it likewise issues no network requests**.
- The service does **not request** the exact-alarm permission (`SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM`); the notification channel name is fixed once, in the system language, when the channel is first created.

⚠️ **Known boundary**: whether the notification **actually appears** from the background isolate is **still unverified on a real device** (no device in this environment; rendering outcome cannot be auto-verified).

**If remote push is ever introduced, we commit to doing three things before it ships**:

1. **Default to the system-level push channel** (Google / Apple), with a payload that **carries no message content**;
2. Offer self-hosted Hub users an **optional self-hosted push path**;
3. **State the data flow party by party** in this Privacy Policy (who sends, who relays, who receives, which fields, how long kept).

> ★ Why: the moment push travels through our servers it re-establishes an "operator ↔ user" data link and
> **breaks the responsibility separation declared in §2.1 and §2.5 of the Terms of Service**.
> This is a design decision, not a wording preference.
