## 1. Minimum age (by region)

The service is intended primarily for adults. It applies a **conservative threshold with per-region enforcement** — better too strict than too loose:

| Region | Minimum age | Basis (directional) |
|---|---|---|
| Global default | **16** | Aligned with the upper end of the GDPR age of consent for information society services (13–16 across member states; the higher value is the safer one) |
| United States | **13** (under 13 is always refused) | COPPA |
| EU (member states that have not lowered it to 13) | **16** | GDPR Art. 8 |
| Jurisdictions requiring a higher age | The local statutory age | Assessed case by case |

> **Why the default is 16 and not 13**: the lower the threshold, the more obligations around children's data pile up (parental consent mechanisms, stricter data minimisation).
> This product has **no child-directed features**, so there is no reason to set the bar low.

## 3. How the age declaration works (engineering side)

| Step | What we do | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Sign-up | Show the age declaration on first launch; the user **must actively confirm** before continuing | `lib/ui/` (onboarding flow, to be wired) |
| Higher-risk features | A second age confirmation before publishing or paying | Same |
| Reporting and deletion | If we learn we received a child's data by mistake, delete it immediately and ban the account | Operations process |

**What we deliberately do not do**: face or ID-based age verification — it directly conflicts with the core value that you should not have to hand over your identity.
The age requirement is therefore **declaratory plus after-the-fact handling**, and users are clearly told the consequences of a false declaration.
